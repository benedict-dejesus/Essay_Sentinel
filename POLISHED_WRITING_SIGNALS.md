# Polished-Writing Follow-Up Signals

## Scope and Boundary

High grammatical accuracy, advanced vocabulary, coherence, and polished structure can all result from skilled human writing, editing support, tutoring, or assistive tools. Essay Sentinel therefore treats these as **visible follow-up observations only**. The app does not convert them into an AI probability, a cheating conclusion, or a writing-quality grade.

| Signal | Deterministic trigger | Educator follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Elevated academic vocabulary density** | At least five matches, including four unique terms, from a visible list of unusually formal academic words. | Ask the student to define or paraphrase selected vocabulary in relation to their evidence. |
| **Ordered cohesion scaffold** | Three or more ordered or formal paragraph-opening cues, such as “firstly,” “secondly,” “finally,” “moreover,” or “in conclusion.” | Ask the student to explain how they planned the order of claims and evidence. |
| **High surface-polish cluster** | At least 200 words, ten complete sentences, no configured mechanics observations, and either elevated vocabulary density or highly regular structure. | Ask about drafting, revisions, tools used, and the origin of the final phrasing. |
| **Regularity marker** | Existing uniform-sentence or highly-regular-paragraph rule matches. | Treat even structure as a discussion prompt, not as a marker of dishonesty. |

## Semantic Coherence Limitation

The static browser version cannot reliably measure “perfect semantics” or true conceptual coherence without a language model or human judgment. It instead reports observable **cohesion scaffolds** and structural regularity. These are intentionally narrower than a claim that the writing has perfect meaning or was assisted by AI.

## Status Rule

A high surface-polish cluster is sufficient to produce **Revision guidance suggested**. Its card explicitly explains the observable combination that triggered it and asks for a process conversation rather than asserting authorship.
